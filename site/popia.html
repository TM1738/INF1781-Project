2. Data ethics, privacy and POPIA

Customer data is not just an asset, it belongs to real people who trusted you with it. In South Africa, the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) sets the rules. It has been fully in force since 1 July 2021 and applies to shops of every size.

What counts as personal information?

Anything that identifies a person: names, ID numbers, phone numbers, email addresses, addresses, purchase history linked to a name, and CCTV footage. Staff records are covered too.

The 8 conditions for lawful processing

ConditionIn your shop
AccountabilityYou, the owner, are responsible. Appoint an Information Officer (usually the owner).
Processing limitationCollect only what you need, with consent or another lawful reason.
Purpose specificationTell people why you collect it, for example loyalty rewards.
Further processingDo not reuse it for something unrelated.
Information qualityKeep it accurate and up to date (see Page 1).
OpennessBe transparent about what you hold.
Security safeguardsPasswords, locked cabinets, backups, and breach notification.
Data subject participationPeople can ask to see, correct or delete their data.

Ethics beyond the law

Ask three questions before using customer data: Would the customer expect this? Would they be comfortable if they saw it? Could it harm them if leaked? Sending a promotion for a product that reveals a sensitive situation, for example, may be legal yet unethical.

Marketing and consent

Unsolicited electronic marketing (SMS, WhatsApp, email) generally needs the person’s opt-in, and every message must give a way to opt out. A customer who bought something once has not agreed to a newsletter.

If something goes wrong

If personal information is accessed by an unauthorised person, you must notify the Information Regulator and affected people as soon as reasonably possible. Penalties can include fines of up to R10 million and, in serious cases, imprisonment.

Common small-shop risks: customer lists on personal WhatsApp groups, shared logins, unlocked paper files, and old staff still having system access.

What to do

  • Register your Information Officer with the Information Regulator.
  • Add a consent line and purpose to your loyalty sign-up form.
  • Give each staff member their own password and remove leavers.
