5. Adapting and customising systems

No package fits perfectly. A spaza-style corner shop, a clothing boutique and a hardware store need different things. The question is how far to bend the software, and how far to bend your processes.

Your options

OptionDescriptionBest when
Use as isOff-the-shelf with default settingsYour needs are common
ConfigureChange settings, fields, reports and tax rulesMost small shops
CustomisePay a developer for changesA unique process gives you an edge
Build your ownBespoke softwareRarely, as it is expensive to maintain
Trade-off: every customisation adds cost, can break during updates and may lock you in. Customise only what makes your business different.

A simple fit check

  1. List your 10 most important tasks.
  2. Mark which the software does out of the box.
  3. For gaps, ask: can we change the process instead?
  4. Customise only what remains and is truly valuable.

Examples for retail

  • Add a field for “supplier lead time” so reorder alerts are realistic.
  • Set VAT (15%) and zero-rated items correctly.
  • Build a dashboard showing only your top five numbers.
  • Support local payment options and multiple languages for till slips.

Managing change

Pilot in one area first, name a staff “champion”, and review after 30 days. Systems succeed when people adopt them, not when they are installed.

What to do

  • Do the fit check on any package you are considering.
