4. Enterprise systems and integration

An enterprise system (often called ERP) joins the main parts of a business, such as sales, stock, purchasing and accounting, around one shared database. Small retailers usually use a lighter version: a point-of-sale (POS) system linked to accounting software.

Why integration matters

Without it, a sale is typed three times: in the till, the stock sheet and the books. Each retype is a chance for error and a delay. With integration, one sale updates everything automatically.

Reading it: a sale at the till updates stock, the books and the dashboard automatically, because they all use the same data.

Integration challenges

ChallengeWhat it looks likeHow to cope
CostMonthly licences, set-up, trainingStart with one module and grow
Data migrationMoving old spreadsheets inClean the data first
Staff resistance“The old way was fine”Involve staff early, train in small steps
Vendor lock-inHard to leave a supplierCheck you can export your data
ConnectivityLoad shedding and weak internetChoose offline-capable POS and a backup power plan

Cloud or on-premises?

Cloud software is paid monthly, updates itself and works from anywhere, but needs internet. On-premises software runs on your own computer, which costs more upfront and needs maintenance. Most small shops do better with cloud.

What to do

  • Draw how one sale moves through your business today and mark every retyping step.
  • Ask two vendors for a trial and a data export option.
